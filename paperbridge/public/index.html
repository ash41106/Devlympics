The gap

Two worlds that should talk every day, but don't.

One side holds the information. The other side holds the expertise. Between them: a pile of paper and a three-week delay.

Side one

📒 The ASHA worker

  • Keeps up to 11 paper registers for every health programme
  • Already juggles 7 apps, WhatsApp groups and spreadsheets
  • Sees danger signs first, but has no doctor to ask
  • Often low signal, sometimes low literacy
21days of delay
Side two

🩺 The district doctor

  • Sees the data only after someone re-types it, weeks later
  • Can't spot an outbreak until it's already spreading
  • Has no fast way to send advice back to the village
  • Can't tell which patient needs help today

How Kaagaz bridges it

Keep the paper. Lose the delay.

We don't ask workers to learn another app. The paper they already keep becomes the input.

1

Snap the page

The worker photographs today's register page. No typing, no forms. Without signal, it waits on the phone and sends itself later.

📷 Register photo · 214 KB
compressed on the phone for 2G
2

AI reads and triages

Gemini reads Hindi and English handwriting, picks out danger signs, and says how sure it is. Anything uncertain goes to a human with the original handwriting beside it.

Help travels back

The doctor sends instructions in one tap. The worker hears them in Hindi and replies by voice. The doctor sees "heard" and "replied", so the loop is closed.
